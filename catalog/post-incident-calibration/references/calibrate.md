# Calibrate

Source: https://howie-guide.pagerduty.com/calibrate/

## What this phase is for

Calibrate is a check before the real meeting, not the meeting itself: share consolidated findings with the people who lived through the incident so an investigator's interpretation can be tested against their actual experience, before it hardens into "the" narrative in front of a bigger audience. It catches misreadings, lets participants clarify or correct specifics, and surfaces which of the themes that emerged actually matter most to prioritize.

## What goes into it

Investigators consolidate everything gathered so far:
- Chat transcripts (Identify)
- Interview notes (Interview)
- Service logs and other technical data (Analyze)
- Raw incident information generally

## Who's involved

- **Investigator(s)**: did the analysis, assembles the document
- **Interviewees and other incident participants**: read it and react -- correcting, clarifying, and helping prioritize

## Building the themes

Themes are the topics of interest that surfaced across the investigation -- pull them into one place (this skill's output document, or a shared doc your team already uses). If there are more themes than a review meeting can reasonably cover, that's normal; the move is to have participants help prioritize down to roughly the top two to five, rather than trying to force everything into one meeting.

## Timelines, plural

It's often worth showing more than one timeline: a version that shows how different practitioners each experienced the incident (since responders frequently have partial, different views in the moment), and a narrative timeline that highlights the key beats -- alerts, escalations, troubleshooting steps taken, and impact.

## The Calibration Document

This is the concrete output of the phase: an interim findings document, not the final incident report. It should include:
- What data was reviewed to produce it (so readers know its basis and its limits)
- Who was interviewed
- The emerging themes identified so far
- Specific prompts inviting feedback, phrased to make it easy for a participant to correct something -- e.g. "I want to make sure I captured this part properly: ..."

Its job is to give the calibration meeting (and whoever facilitates it) something concrete and falsifiable to react to, rather than opening the meeting with a blank page.

## What comes after (out of scope for this skill)

Calibrate ends with the document ready to circulate. Actually facilitating the calibration/learning-review meeting where participants react to it, and writing the final incident report afterward, are later stages of a full post-incident process that this skill doesn't cover -- see `SKILL.md` for why that boundary is drawn here.
